# Learning Content Authority Policy

This policy defines who owns Course, Module, Theme, Lesson, plans, and materials.

## Academic hierarchy

`Course → Module → Theme → Lesson`

Each level has one primary academic owner. Lower-level owners may adapt implementation inside the approved parent frame but may not silently redefine the parent outcome.

## Ownership

- **Course — Dean.** Owns name, direction, target competencies, prerequisites, Module framework, Course Success Criteria, and canonical Course learning route.
- **Module — Dean.** Owns Module purpose, boundaries, dependencies, Module Success Criteria, placement/skip/bridge decision, and initial Module coverage plan. Subject specialists contribute before approval.
- **Theme frame — Dean; Theme teaching design — Lecturer.** The Dean owns and approves the Theme outcome, Success Criteria, exclusions, finite Lesson map, final checkpoint, and stop condition. The Lecturer owns the teaching design and materials of that Theme inside the approved frame.
- **Lesson — Lecturer.** Owns preparation and delivery design for a lesson that introduces new material inside the Dean-approved Theme slot. A Lesson has an explicit outcome and is created just in time for the current Student/context; it cannot add a slot or change Theme criteria.
- **Reinforcement session — Teacher.** Owns interactive consolidation/remediation sessions after or alongside teaching. Teacher does not silently create new Course/Module scope.
- **Lab — Laboratory Specialist.** Owns practical/laboratory activity design aligned with Theme/Module criteria.
- **Independent measurement — Learning Analyst.** Owns assessment design and evidence within the assigned criteria, not learning content.
- **Module examination — Examiner.** Owns final examination design and verdict against approved Module Success Criteria, not teaching content.

## Approval versus contribution

Ownership means accountability for the canonical decision, not that the owner must write every artifact alone.

A Dean may ask Lecturer, Lab Specialist, Learning Analyst, and Examiner for Module-design input. A Lecturer may ask Teacher/Lab Specialist for Theme or Lesson input. Research and authoring Skills may be used by any Profession that has them, but Skills do not own academic scope.

## Student response and material feedback

A Student response to a Lesson has two strictly separate interpretations:

- **Learning evidence** — the Lecturer evaluates the Student's demonstrated
  knowledge and understanding against the Lesson criteria. This may inform
  teaching adaptation but is not automatically a mastery or assessment verdict.
- **Material feedback** — the Instructional Assistant extracts the Student's
  explicit criticism, confusion signals, and clarity observations, then reviews
  the complete Lesson for pedagogical coherence.

Student evidence is interpreted semantically. The suggested evidence structure
helps the Student communicate, but headings, order, exact wording, and exact
example values are not independent requirements when the response clearly
demonstrates the intended understanding. The Lecturer asks for clarification
only when a required concept is missing, contradictory, or genuinely unclear.

The Student's criticism is input to review, not authority over the material.
The Student MUST NOT directly edit, approve, reject, publish, expand, narrow, or
redefine a Lesson, Theme, Module, Course, success criterion, or academic
workflow. The Instructional Assistant MUST NOT edit the material or assess the
Student's knowledge. The Lecturer alone decides whether to revise the Lesson
and alone performs the revision within the approved scope.

If a proposed change affects Theme, Module, Course, placement, or assessment
authority, the Lecturer must refer it to the owning Dean or other authorized
Profession instead of accepting the Student request directly.

## Submission continuation

Student submission is a workflow handoff. The responsible Lecturer must
process the evidence, communicate the current learning conclusion and next
step, and retain ownership until a concrete bounded handoff is made. No Worker
is invoked merely because a response was submitted. The next Profession is
selected only for a demonstrated need and receives an explicit purpose, scope,
criteria, and permitted context.

## Parent-frame rule

A child level must inherit and serve its parent:

- Module serves Course competencies.
- Theme serves Module Success Criteria.
- Lesson serves Theme outcome.
- Exercise/material/lab serves Lesson, Theme, or Module criteria explicitly.

If a lower-level Worker discovers that the parent frame is wrong or incomplete, they propose a change to the parent owner instead of silently expanding scope.

## Just-in-time rule

Do not fully author Themes, Lessons, exercises, labs, or materials for the whole Course in advance. Prepare the next useful level when the Student is approaching it and current evidence is available.

## Finite Theme rule

Just-in-time Lesson authoring does not mean an open-ended Theme. Before a Theme
starts, the Dean must approve its finite Lesson sequence, Theme outcome,
Success Criteria, scope exclusions, final checkpoint, and stop condition. The
Lecturer may prepare the approved Lessons just in time, but may not add a
Lesson, extend the sequence, or keep producing material after the checkpoint.
Any unresolved gap or scope change returns to Dean for a new bounded decision.
