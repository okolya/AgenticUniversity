# Learning Content Authority Policy

This policy defines who owns Course, Module, Theme, Lesson, plans, and materials.

## Academic hierarchy

`Course → Module → Theme → Lesson`

Each level has one primary academic owner. Lower-level owners may adapt implementation inside the approved parent frame but may not silently redefine the parent outcome.

## Ownership

- **Course — Dean.** Owns name, direction, target competencies, prerequisites, Module framework, Course Success Criteria, and canonical Course learning route.
- **Module — Dean.** Owns Module purpose, boundaries, dependencies, Module Success Criteria, placement/skip/bridge decision, and initial Module coverage plan. Subject specialists contribute before approval.
- **Theme — Lecturer.** Owns the academic teaching design of a Theme inside the approved Module: Theme goal, concepts/skills to introduce, Theme Success Criteria proposal, lesson sequence, and required learning materials. The Theme must remain inside Module scope.
- **Lesson — Lecturer.** Owns preparation and delivery design for a lesson that introduces new material. A lesson has an explicit outcome and is created just in time for the current Student/context.
- **Reinforcement session — Teacher.** Owns interactive consolidation/remediation sessions after or alongside teaching. Teacher does not silently create new Course/Module scope.
- **Lab — Laboratory Specialist.** Owns practical/laboratory activity design aligned with Theme/Module criteria.
- **Independent measurement — Learning Analyst.** Owns assessment design and evidence within the assigned criteria, not learning content.
- **Module examination — Examiner.** Owns final examination design and verdict against approved Module Success Criteria, not teaching content.

## Approval versus contribution

Ownership means accountability for the canonical decision, not that the owner must write every artifact alone.

A Dean may ask Lecturer, Lab Specialist, Learning Analyst, and Examiner for Module-design input. A Lecturer may ask Teacher/Lab Specialist for Theme or Lesson input. Research and authoring Skills may be used by any Profession that has them, but Skills do not own academic scope.

## Parent-frame rule

A child level must inherit and serve its parent:

- Module serves Course competencies.
- Theme serves Module Success Criteria.
- Lesson serves Theme outcome.
- Exercise/material/lab serves Lesson, Theme, or Module criteria explicitly.

If a lower-level Worker discovers that the parent frame is wrong or incomplete, they propose a change to the parent owner instead of silently expanding scope.

## Just-in-time rule

Do not fully author Themes, Lessons, exercises, labs, or materials for the whole Course in advance. Prepare the next useful level when the Student is approaching it and current evidence is available.
