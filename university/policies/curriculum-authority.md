# Curriculum Authority Policy

## Ownership

A Course belongs to a Faculty. The appointed Dean of that Faculty owns the Course architecture and its academic approval.

Course architecture includes:
- Course name and direction;
- Course goal and target competencies;
- prerequisites;
- Module framework and ordering/dependencies;
- Course-level Success Criteria;
- rules for Module entry, skipping, bridging, and completion.

## Contributions are not ownership

The Dean may request contributions from appointed specialists:
- Lecturer contributes subject/domain expertise and proposes academically sound coverage;
- Learning Analyst contributes measurable competency and assessment perspective;
- Examiner contributes examinability and Module-exit perspective;
- Laboratory Specialist may contribute practical capability requirements;
- `research-materials` may gather current external evidence and references.

Contributors do not independently change the canonical Course. The Dean approves changes to Course architecture.

## Just-in-time rule

A Course is a route of intended competencies, not a fully pre-authored syllabus.

The Course may define the Module framework in advance. Detailed Module themes, lessons, exercises, labs, materials, and assessment artifacts are created or refined near Module entry using:
- the Student's verified current state;
- results of prior Modules;
- current Course goals and Success Criteria;
- relevant changes in the field;
- available high-quality materials.

Do not pre-generate detailed content merely to make the Course look complete.

## Boundary

- Rector governs Faculties but does not design Faculty Courses unless separately appointed into an academic role that has that authority.
- Dean owns curriculum architecture but does not replace Lecturer teaching, Learning Analyst evidence, or Examiner verdicts.
- Student participates in goals and planning but does not silently mutate the canonical public Course definition.

## Content-level delegation

Course ownership does not mean the Dean authors every Theme or Lesson. Detailed authority is defined in `learning-content-authority.md`:
- Dean owns Course and Module frames and approves the finite Theme frame;
- Lecturer owns assigned Theme teaching design and new-material Lesson design inside the approved Theme frame;
- Teacher owns reinforcement/remediation sessions;
- Laboratory Specialist owns labs/practical sessions;
- Learning Analyst owns independent measurement against supplied criteria;
- Examiner owns final Module examination/verdict.

## Module competency contracts

Before detailed Module content exists, the Dean owns a stable competency boundary for each Module: purpose, Entry Contract, Exit Contract, broad competency areas, and hard dependencies. See `module-contracts.md`.

A Course therefore can be academically navigable before its teaching materials exist. Navigation is based on verified competencies and contracts, not on mandatory consumption of previous content.
