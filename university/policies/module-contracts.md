# Module Competency Contracts Policy

## Principle

A Module is connected to the Course by competency contracts, not by mandatory consumption of the preceding Module.

Every Module must have a public **Entry Contract** and **Exit Contract** before detailed Themes, Lessons, exercises, labs, or materials are authored.

## Entry Contract

The Entry Contract states the minimum competencies/evidence needed for the Module to be educationally meaningful. It is expressed as observable capabilities, not as attendance requirements such as "completed Module 2" or "read Lessons 1-7".

Entry criteria should be detailed enough that a Learning Analyst can independently verify them, but no more detailed than needed to establish readiness.

## Exit Contract

The Exit Contract is the Module-level Success Criteria. It states observable capabilities the Student must demonstrate at Module completion. It defines the destination without prescribing the teaching path.

Exit criteria should be detailed enough for an Examiner to design a defensible final verification, but must not pre-author Themes, Lessons, exercises, labs, or materials.

## Competency areas

A Module frame may name broad competency areas to communicate scope. Competency areas are boundaries, not a hidden syllabus.

## Relationship between Modules

The Exit Contract of Module N does not have to equal the Entry Contract of Module N+1. A Module may produce broader competencies than later Modules require. A Student may enter any Module when its Entry Contract is satisfied by current verified evidence and Course dependencies permit it.

Therefore:
- prior Module completion is evidence, not the universal prerequisite;
- Learning Analyst verifies competencies when evidence is missing, stale, contradictory, or uncertain;
- Dean owns the placement/skip/bridge decision;
- missing Entry competencies may be addressed by a bounded bridge rather than forcing consumption of an entire prior Module.

## Detail boundary

Before Student-specific Module Entry, Dean may define only:
- Module purpose/value;
- Entry Contract;
- Exit Contract;
- broad competency areas;
- hard dependencies, if any.

Dean should not pre-author merely for completeness:
- detailed Theme sequence;
- Lesson sequence;
- teaching explanations;
- exercises or labs;
- exact learning resources;
- assessment artifacts.

Those are designed just in time after the actual Student starting state is known.


## Public Module versus Student Module Enrollment

A public University Module is a durable academic object. It is never marked completed or closed because one Student finishes it. Its contract, accumulated materials, reusable labs, methods, and teaching experience may continue to evolve.

Completion belongs to the private **Student Module Enrollment / Module Learning Plan**. A Student enrollment may progress through learning, examination, post-examination application/certification activity, and completion without changing the lifecycle state of the public Module.

`EXAM_PASSED` and `STUDENT_MODULE_COMPLETED` are distinct states. Passing the internal examination confirms the Exit Contract. The Student Module Enrollment is completed only when all activities required by that Student's agreed Module plan are completed, waived, or formally resolved.
