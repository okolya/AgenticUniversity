# Між B1 та B2

- faculty: Language Faculty
- owner: appointed Dean of Language Faculty
- status: public architecture approved; Themes and Lessons not yet authored
- language: German
- route type: bounded bridge to readiness for a separate B2 course
- pace: adaptive to Student capacity and verified evidence

## Course purpose

Restore the German competencies required to begin a B2 language course with
confidence after a long interruption of study. The Course identifies and
repairs only the gaps that block reliable B1 performance, then verifies
readiness for the next B2 course.

This Course is not a B2 course. It does not teach B2 competencies, replace a
future University B2 Course, or require a fixed number of hours or Lessons.

## Course route

1. **A2 Recovery** — selectively restore prerequisite A2 capabilities when
   entry evidence shows that they block B1 work.
2. **B1 Recovery** — consolidate B1 comprehension, production, grammar,
   vocabulary, and interaction, with targeted correction of recurring gaps.
3. **Practice and Readiness Check** — apply B1 competencies across Lesen,
   Hören, Schreiben, and Sprechen; identify remaining gaps, correct them, and
   recheck the evidence.
4. **B2 Entry Readiness** — verify that the Student can enter a B2 course
   without teaching the B2 curriculum itself.

Practice is distributed through every Module. Each bounded learning cycle
uses explanation or modeling only as needed, guided practice, independent
application, evidence check, and targeted correction or progression.

## Entry Contract

The Student may enter this Course when:

1. German study has previously reached approximately the A2/B1 range, or the
   Student requests a restart after a substantial interruption.
2. The Student can provide a small sample of current German performance, or
   accepts an initial diagnostic before placement inside the Course.
3. The Student agrees that the route is evidence-led: a demonstrated
   competency is not retaught merely to fill a Module.

These are placement conditions, not claims that the Student currently
demonstrates the level.

Entry is placement, not an admission barrier. The entry diagnostic shows where
in the fixed four-Module route a Student begins and which criteria are narrowed
or skipped. A Student is turned away only when the Course Entry Contract is not
met (for example, a level far outside the A2/B1 range), and then the Dean
offers another route instead of a bare refusal.

## Course Success Criteria

At the end of the Course, the Student independently demonstrates B1 readiness
in all four skills:

1. understand the main points and relevant details of clear standard German
   on familiar everyday, study, and work-related topics;
2. participate in routine conversations and handle common situations with
   comprehensible, coherent language;
3. write connected practical texts with an understandable structure, adequate
   vocabulary, and controlled B1 grammar;
4. read and extract the main information and relevant details from B1-level
   texts;
5. recognize and repair the recurring A2/B1 grammar, vocabulary, and
   word-order errors that previously blocked communication;
6. use learning and correction strategies to notice uncertainty, request
   clarification, and revise a spoken or written attempt;
7. complete a bounded readiness check whose result supports entry into a
   separate B2 course.

The Course does not require the Student to demonstrate B2 abilities such as
routine comprehension of complex abstract texts, fluent native-speaker
conversation, or detailed current-issue argumentation. Those belong to the
next B2 course.

## Module framework

### 1. A2 Recovery

Value: restore only the A2 foundations that the diagnostic shows are blocking
reliable B1 performance.

### 2. B1 Recovery

Value: rebuild and stabilize the B1 competencies needed for independent
everyday communication and entry into B2 study.

### 3. Practice and Readiness Check

Value: turn recovered knowledge into repeatable performance across all four
skills, then use evidence to correct remaining gaps and decide readiness.

### 4. B2 Entry Readiness

Value: verify the transition boundary and produce a clear handoff into a
separate B2 course without teaching that Course's content.

## Module navigation rules

- **Order and dependencies:** the Modules are listed in the recommended route
  order. There are no hard dependencies between Modules; a Student may enter any
  Module whose Entry Contract is satisfied by current verified evidence. How
  recent the evidence must be is set by the Learning Analyst for each Entry
  check.
- **Entry:** Entry Contracts state observable competencies. Completion of an
  earlier Module is evidence, never a prerequisite in itself.
- **Skip or narrow:** the Dean may skip a Module, or narrow it to the criteria
  that current evidence shows are not yet stable. Narrowed criteria are the only
  ones taught and examined for that Student.
- **Bridge:** a missing Entry competency is addressed by a bounded bridge
  decided by the Dean, not by consuming a whole earlier Module.
- **Return:** when evidence at any Module shows an unmet earlier criterion, the
  Dean opens a targeted return to the relevant Module or bridge instead of
  extending the current Module.
- **Completion:** the public Module is never closed. Completion belongs to the
  private Student Module Enrollment, as defined in `module-contracts.md`.

## Evidence delta by Module

Each Module must add evidence the previous one did not; demonstrated criteria
are not re-measured merely to fill a Module.

| Module | New evidence it must produce |
|---|---|
| 1. A2 Recovery | closure of the diagnosed A2 gaps that blocked B1 work |
| 2. B1 Recovery | stable B1 competencies in familiar contexts, with recurring personal errors under control |
| 3. Practice and Readiness Check | independent B1 performance across all four skills in new but bounded contexts, plus revision after feedback |
| 4. B2 Entry Readiness | B1 transfer under light novelty and the Student's own account of remaining limitations, as input to the Dean's B2-entry decision |

## Outcome strategy

- External certification: `none`. The Goethe-Zertifikat materials are boundary
  references only; no certification is adopted as an outcome of this Course.
- Professional application: `none`. Practical transfer is built into the
  Modules' four-skill tasks; no separate post-examination Application Plan is
  required by this Course. The Dean may revisit this if a real Student context
  or an adopted certification changes the need.

## Calibration of Exit criteria

Exit criteria use qualitative wording on purpose. They are interpreted against
the B1 descriptors named in Source provenance. The concrete tasks, thresholds,
and evidence rules are defined by the appointed Examiner and Learning Analyst in
their assessment artifacts, within the Course Success Criteria, and are not
fixed in this public frame.

## Delivery boundary

This Course defines public competency and Module frames. Detailed Themes,
finite Lesson maps, exercises, and teaching materials may be prepared ahead for
Dean-approved Theme frames and carry an `unpiloted` mark until a Student has
passed them. Delivery to a Student starts only after Course Entry, and each
Lesson is confirmed against that Student's verified evidence.
The Dean owns Course and Module contracts. An appointed Lecturer owns
Theme/Lesson teaching design; a Teacher owns reinforcement; and a Learning
Analyst owns independent measurement when that workflow is staffed.

The Language Faculty has appointed Lecturer, Teacher, Laboratory Specialist,
Learning Analyst, and Examiner Workers (see `university/faculties/language/STAFF.md`).
Delivery to a Student still begins only after Course Entry, and the
independence rules of `assessment-independence.md`
apply to every assessed scope.
